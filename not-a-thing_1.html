The Advertising Wars

Subch. 4-7, rewritten eff. 5/1/2013  ·  Fla. Bar v. Went For It, 515 U.S. 618 (1995)
The Bar's own rules go from protecting the public against advertising to protecting lawyers' ability to compete.
  • 1989–90 — the Bar studies advertising's effects for two years, then bans direct-mail solicitation of accident victims for 30 days.
  • 1995 — the U.S. Supreme Court upholds the ban, calling lawyer ads "commercial speech" entitled to only limited protection.
  • 2013 — the Bar rewrites Subchapter 4-7 almost entirely, loosening restrictions it had defended less than two decades earlier. Justice Pariente dissents.

The Website Problem

Rule 4-7.6  →  Rule 4-7.20(g)
A law firm website is advertising, filed and reviewed for a fee information a client sought out, exempt from filing.
  • For years, nobody agreed whether a firm's own website counted as "advertising" subject to filing and a review fee.
  • The fix: websites are substantively regulated, but exempt from the filing requirement, because a website is sought out, not sent.
  • A doctrine built for print and broadcast had to invent a new category just to survive contact with the internet.

Lawyers on Social Media

Guidelines for Networking Sites  ·  Standing Comm. on Advertising, rev. 2020
A lawyer's LinkedIn page is one thing three things at once — a website, a set of testimonials, and, if boosted, a paid ad.
  • A lawyer's own page, if used to promote the practice, is treated like a website: exempt from filing, but still bound by the substantive advertising rules.
  • A client's "recommendation" on the page counts as a testimonial — the same restricted category as a client quote in a print ad.
  • A paid, "boosted" post is treated differently again: like a traditional ad, it has to be filed for review before it runs.

The Text Message

Subch. 4-7  ·  direct-contact solicitation rules
A text to an accident victim is unthinkable permitted, if it opens with the word "Advertisement".
  • The 1990, mail-based, 30-day rule now has to govern a medium invented decades later.
  • A qualifying text must open with "Advertisement," disclose how the lawyer got the number, and tell the recipient to disregard it if already represented.
  • The channel changed. The disclosure architecture built for paper did not.
2010s
Economics

Who Owns the Firm?

Rule 4-5.4, amended 2022
A nonlawyer's equity stake in a firm is worth a pilot program rejected, 46 to 0.
  • 2021 — the Bar's own special committee recommends a Utah-style sandbox letting nonlawyers hold minority equity in firms.
  • The Board of Governors rejects it unanimously — 46-0 on ownership, 45-0 on fee-splitting with nonlawyers. The Court agrees.
  • Arizona and Utah move the opposite direction in the same years. Same economic pressure, opposite answer.

The App That Practiced Law

Fla. Bar v. TIKD Services, LLC, 326 So. 3d 1073 (Fla. 2021)
Matching a driver to a lawyer for a flat fee is a business proposition the unauthorized practice of law — 4 to 3.
  • Upload a ticket, pay a flat fee, get matched with a lawyer, get refunded if you lose. The Bar's own referee found no violation.
  • The Florida Supreme Court reverses, 4-3.
  • Justice Couriel, dissenting: TIKD offered "not legal services, but a business proposition."

Competence

Know Your Tools

Rule 4-1.1, cmt.  ·  Rule 6-10.3
Technological competence is optional, a matter of personal style mandatory — three hours, every three years.
  • 2016 — the comment to the competence rule is amended: competence now includes understanding technology's risks and benefits.
  • The same year, Florida becomes the first mandatory bar in the country to require CLE hours specifically in technology.
  • The pressure came from e-discovery and cloud storage — not from anything resembling AI.

Competence

Learning Machines, Learning Rules

Eth. Op. 24-1 (Jan. 19, 2024)  →  SC2024-0032 (eff. Oct. 28, 2024)
A lawyer's duty to review AI output is unwritten the same duty owed to a paralegal.
  • January 2024 — an advisory opinion, unanimously approved, says lawyers may use generative AI if they protect confidentiality and verify the output.
  • By October 2024, the advisory becomes binding rule.
  • The fix borrows old doctrine for a new tool: a lawyer's oversight of AI is modeled on the decades-old duty to supervise a paralegal.
